# Staff behavioral exercises

Answer each prompt in **Situation, Task, Action, Result** format. Include
scale, dissent, your decision or influence, measurable outcome, and the durable
change that remained after the incident or project.

1. A team wants to lower DNS TTL globally during an outage. Explain how you
   challenged the shortcut and aligned on a safer containment plan.
2. Two teams disagree about who owns a failing DNS/IPAM workflow. Establish
   an ownership contract and show how you repaired the relationship.
3. You must persuade teams to adopt a common edge platform while preserving
   valid product-specific exceptions. Describe the adoption sequence and proof.
4. A migration improves availability but raises cross-zone cost. Explain how
   you made the trade-off visible and reached a decision.
5. An incident commander asks for a risky production experiment. Explain how
   you protected customers, preserved evidence, and kept the investigation moving.
6. A senior engineer rejects your multi-region design as too complex. Describe
   how you incorporated useful criticism or changed your position.
7. A postmortem identifies a familiar individual mistake. Explain how you
   redirected the group toward system controls without minimizing accountability.
8. A platform team cannot meet every onboarding request. Explain prioritization,
   a transparent queue, and the metric that showed the policy was working.
9. A certificate migration has unknown consumers. Explain how you built an
   inventory, created overlap, and communicated the remaining uncertainty.
10. A direct report proposes a technically correct but unsafe network change.
    Explain how you coached them and improved the review process.
11. A cross-team SLO has a disputed denominator. Explain how you resolved the
    definition and handled teams whose reported reliability changed.
12. A project is successful technically but adoption is low. Explain what you
    learned, what you changed, and how you measured renewed adoption.

## Review checklist

- **Scope:** Did the story establish users, scale, and authority?
- **Influence:** Did the candidate change a decision or behavior without relying
  only on hierarchy?
- **Learning:** Did new evidence change the plan?
- **Outcome:** Is there a measurable customer or engineering result?
- **Durability:** Did the system, process, or ownership model improve?
